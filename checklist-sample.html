What is inside the Integrity Checklist

The checklist is a 9-page PDF with 28 checks for a backtest. Each check says what goes wrong, gives a test you can run, and has a pass or fail box. A scoring sheet at the end tells you when one failure is enough to stop. This page lists all 28 and prints three of them in full, so you can judge it before paying.

Who it is for: people who run their own backtests, in TradingView's Strategy Tester or in code. Who it is not for: if you only read an indicator by eye and never test it, most of the 28 will not apply to you. The free repaint checker is the better start.

All 28 checks

Data (5)

  • Survivorship bias
  • Data that was not available at the time
  • Bad ticks, gaps and missing sessions
  • Split and dividend errors
  • Timestamp and session conventions

Logic (5)

  • Same-bar lookahead
  • Repainting indicators
  • Higher-timeframe leakage
  • Stop and target inside one bar
  • Position-accounting bugs

Execution (5)

  • Fills that could not happen
  • Slippage and spread ignored
  • Fees, commissions and financing
  • Size larger than the market traded
  • Short availability and margin

Statistics (5)

  • Overfitting and fragile parameters
  • Sample too small
  • Testing many variants and reading the best
  • No out-of-sample or walk-forward test
  • A hand-picked test window

Regime (4)

  • One market regime mistaken for many
  • Dependence on volatility
  • Structural breaks
  • Other positions ignored

Process (4)

  • Runs you cannot reproduce
  • Judging on total return alone
  • No benchmark
  • Backtest and live engine differ

Three checks in full

Repainting indicators

Some indicators recompute their history. ZigZag, fractals, some support and resistance tools and anything that calls request.security the wrong way show signals on old bars that were never visible while those bars were live.

Test: watch the indicator live for a week and screenshot each signal as it appears. Then compare with the same period after a reload. A signal that moved, appeared or vanished is a repaint. In Pine, look for lookahead=barmerge.lookahead_on, logic that depends on calc_on_every_tick, and references to the bar that has not closed yet.

Pass: live signals match the history. Fail: anything else.

Slippage and spread ignored

A backtest that crosses the spread for free on every trade overstates every result, and the more often a system trades the bigger the gap.

Test: add slippage on each side: at least one tick on liquid futures, at least half the spread plus some impact on stocks, more around the open, the close and news. Run it at 1x, 2x and 3x your estimate and plot the average result per trade against slippage. If the line crosses zero inside a plausible range, it fails.

Pass: still positive at twice a realistic slippage.

Sample too small

Thirty trades say very little. The range around the average result per trade usually still includes zero.

Test: with N closed trades, a rough range is the average result plus or minus 2 times the standard deviation of results, divided by the square root of N. If that range contains zero, there is no evidence yet. If you can, resample the trades 10,000 times and report the 5th percentile of the total.

Pass: the range excludes zero with about 100 trades or more, depending on context.

Where this fails

  • A passed checklist means the test was run honestly. It does not mean the strategy will make money, and nothing can show that in advance.
  • Several checks (survivorship, data that was not available at the time, short availability) are written for stock and futures universes. On a single TradingView chart some will be "not applicable", and the sheet asks you to write why.
  • The checks need you to run the tests. The PDF does not run anything for you.
  • It has no reviews yet: nobody has bought it.
