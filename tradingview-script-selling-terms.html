TradingView's revised rules for selling invite-only scripts, summarised

TradingView changed the rules for selling invite-only scripts twice in six weeks. This page summarises what its own announcements and terms say as of 8 November 2026, in order, so a script author or a buyer can see what is in force and what is dated for later. It is a reading of public text, not legal advice, and it says nothing about whether selling scripts is a good idea.

The short version

  • The plan announced in September, that paid invite-only scripts could only be sold through TradingView's Marketplace from 1 November, did not take effect. TradingView's 6 November post says so directly.
  • Authors can keep selling the way they did: on their own website, in a course or in a community, then grant access on TradingView. They can also sell through a Marketplace "Paid Space", or do both.
  • What is new for own-channel sales is a seat limit, starting 1 February 2027.
  • Private invite-only scripts have their own limits and two dates: 15 November and 1 December 2026.

Two ways to sell, and one rule they share

Own channels. Section 23.1 of the Terms of Service says an author may grant other users access to invite-only scripts, "including for payment you collect through your own channels." Section 23.6 adds that when you charge outside the Marketplace you are responsible for sales, pricing, payments, refunds, taxes and customer support, and that TradingView is not a party to those transactions.

Marketplace Paid Space. Customers pay on TradingView, access is granted automatically and the script appears in public feeds. The platform fee is currently 0%, with advance notice promised before any change. There is no joining deadline; applicants need a Premium or higher plan and at least one public invite-only script, and approval is selective. Some features (annual billing, price tiers, promo codes, free trials, reviews) are described as "being worked on" and are not available yet.

Both are allowed together. Marketplace customers do not use seats.

The shared rule. The script being sold has to be a public invite-only script, and moderators review it under the House Rules. TradingView's Vendor Requirements add that access instructions go in the "Author's instructions" field, that prices can be mentioned only there or in the profile Signature, and that public posts cannot be used to advertise a paid script, apart from how-to content with a "HOW-TO:" title.

What "seats" mean

From 1 February 2027, own-channel sales are counted in seats: one per person while their access is active, across all of an author's scripts. A Premium or higher plan includes 100 seats, and each extra 100 seats costs $300 a month. Access granted before 1 February, including lifetime access, is kept and, in TradingView's words, "never charged for unless you extend it or add a script." If an author's seats are full, existing users keep access; the author just cannot add or extend anyone until seats free up. Protected scripts do not take seats, and neither do Marketplace customers.

Private invite-only scripts

Private invite-only scripts are for internal use: up to 5 users per script and 5 new private scripts a month, and their users take seats too. Sharing a private script in any way, including advertising it on a website, makes it public and subject to moderation. The Vendor Requirements page says the same.

For buyers

Nothing a buyer paid for before the change is taken away. Marketplace buyers pay with the same methods used for TradingView plans; own-channel buyers deal with the author, not TradingView, for payment and refunds.

Where it fails: this summary

  • It is a summary of a moving target. TradingView says it may change the Marketplace fee with notice, the Access API is not live, and the terms page shows no revision date. Check the linked source text before acting on anything here.
  • It is not legal or tax advice. Section 23.6 puts taxes, refunds and customer support on the author for own-channel sales; this page does not tell anyone how to handle them.
  • It covers invite-only scripts only. Open-source and protected scripts are always free and sit outside the seat rules.
  • It does not judge the options. Whether the seat cost, the Marketplace's selective approval or neither is worth it depends on numbers this page does not have.
  • Secondhand reports differ. Some outlets reported the 1 November plan as in force; the 6 November post is the later and primary text.
