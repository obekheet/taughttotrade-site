Market Structure Break & Order Block, explained from the source

"Market Structure Break & Order Block" by EmreKb is one of the most-boosted free scripts on TradingView, with more than 64,000 boosts. It draws a ZigZag, writes "MSB" when a swing breaks the one before it, and shades boxes on candles it calls order blocks and breaker blocks. The swings and the MSB labels are confirmed some bars after the turn and then drawn back at it, and when a box breaks, the code removes the oldest box in its list, which is often not the one that broke.

How it works

  1. ZigZag swings. The direction flips to up when a bar makes a new 9-bar high (ZigZag Length 9), and to down on a new 9-bar low. At each flip, the extreme of the leg just finished becomes a swing point, and the ZigZag line is drawn back to it.
  2. Market structure break (MSB). A bearish MSB is printed when the newest swing low is below the previous swing low by more than 0.33 times the last leg (Fib Factor 0.33). A bullish MSB is the mirror image with swing highs. The line and the "MSB" label are placed at the swing, in the past.
  3. Order block (OB). At a bullish MSB, the last down candle in the swing before the break is boxed as "Bu-OB" (the last up candle for a bearish one).
  4. Breaker / mitigation block (BB / MB). The last opposite candle one swing further back gets a second box. It is labelled breaker if the new swing went beyond the old one, mitigation if not.
  5. Boxes stay and extend 10 bars past the current bar until the close goes through them.

Why it looks better in hindsight

Every swing is known only after price has moved 9 bars' worth the other way, and the MSB needs that swing to exist. In our simulation the MSB arrived a median of 5 to 6 bars after the swing extreme it is drawn at. On a finished chart, the labels and boxes sit neatly at turning points; at the time, you would have seen them later, with price already away from the level. More in what repainting is.

Two things in the code worth knowing

The wrong box can disappear. With "Delete Old/Broken Boxes" on (the default), a box that the close breaks through calls a delete routine that removes the first box in that list, the oldest, not the box that was broken. In our simulation, in 50 to 60% of breaks an older, still-unbroken box was removed instead, and the broken one stayed until it reached the front of the list. A zone can vanish from your chart without price ever touching it.

The bearish order-block alert fires above the zone. The zone loops for the other three box types say "if broken... else if inside... alert". The bearish OB loop drops the "else", so "Price in the BE-OB zone" also fires when the close is above the box, after it has broken. In our simulation about 1 in 5 of those alerts came with the close above the zone.

The settings

The alerts

There is one alert condition you can pick in the alert dialog: MSB, when the structure flips. The four zone messages ("Price in the BU-OB zone" and so on) come from alert() calls inside the script: they need an "Any alert() function call" alert and fire on every bar the close sits in a zone, not once per touch. None of them says anything about what price does next.

Where it fails

  • Late, drawn early. Swings, MSB lines and labels are confirmed after the turn and drawn back at it (median 5-6 bars in our simulation).
  • The wrong box gets deleted. Breaking one box removes the oldest one in its list; unbroken zones can vanish.
  • The bearish OB alert fires above the zone, after the zone has broken.
  • Zone alerts repeat every bar while the close is inside, so a slow drift through a box sends many alerts.
  • One candle defines a zone. The box is the full high-low range of a single candle chosen by colour, so a doji or a tiny candle can be the "order block".
  • Fixed thresholds. 9 bars and 0.33 apply the same way on every market and timeframe; there is no test here of whether any break or zone meant anything.
